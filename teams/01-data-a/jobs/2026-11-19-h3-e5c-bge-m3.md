## Why

On FLORES-200 (`h3_labse_check_results_v1.md`, `h3_bge_m3_check_results_v1.md`), at sentence threshold 0.7:

| Pair | LaBSE | BGE-M3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| sa–en, translated blocks qualifying | 29% | 82% |
| sa–hi, translated blocks qualifying | 19% | 95% |
| hi–en, or–en | 100% | 100% |
| Unrelated blocks qualifying | 0% | 0% |
| Best wrong match, median cosine | 0.41 | 0.57 |
| Encoding time (laptop CPU, same check) | 863 s | 7,255 s |

LaBSE needs a lower Sanskrit threshold (E5c uses 0.55, provisional), where same-topic pages start to qualify (3 of
7 real-page matches at 0.5 are not translations). BGE-M3 reaches Sanskrit at 0.7, but scores unrelated text higher and
is about 8 times slower. FLORES is clean, sentence-aligned text; this job measures both encoders on the real sample, so
E5d's readers can label one set of candidates and TSAI can choose the encoder and threshold from the numbers.

## Time

About 314,000 of the 383,164 documents in scope are long enough to compare (E5c's smoke estimate: en 217,552, hi 91,153,
sa 5,694), each encoded once (its first 4 sentences, at most 128 tokens). BGE-M3 is XLM-RoBERTa-large (24 layers, 1,024
wide), about 3–4 times LaBSE's compute per token at the same length, and on the FLORES check it took 8.4 times as long
on CPU. No CUDA rate for either encoder exists yet (E5c's will be the first). On the same laptop CPU, BGE-M3 encoded 1.0 document heads/s (smoke run) against LaBSE's 4.1–6.7 (E5c's smoke run), so expect this job's encode stage to take about 4–7 times E5c's. The config stops at 170 minutes with
`status: stopped_time_limit` and a summary of each stage's rate. If it stops, running the same command again continues
from the embedding cache (`out/h3_cache_bge_m3/`, kept per part), so only the unfinished part is encoded again; or the
fallback is a narrower rerun without `en-nemotroncc`, sized from that summary.
