## Why

H3 (`h3_design_v1.md`) is the same content in another language. It was out of scope for v1 (TSAI, 5 Nov). E5a′
checked the encoder on FLORES-200 (`h3_labse_check_results_v1.md`, `h3_bge_m3_check_results_v1.md`); E5c is the first
count on the real sample. Its pairs at and below the thresholds are what E5d's readers label, and its counts are the
evidence for TSAI's ruling on whether H3 removes anything.

## Time

Of the 417,176 documents in scope, about 348,000 are long enough to compare (the smoke run's estimate on the local sample: en 217,552, hi 91,153, or 33,630, sa 5,694; shorter ones are counted and never encoded). Each is encoded once (its first 4 sentences), and only pairs that pass retrieval are
verified sentence by sentence (at most 10,000). Measured rates: 37.9 document heads/s on an Apple GPU (fp32,
`logs/2026-11-17-h3-local.txt`) and 4.1–6.7 on a laptop CPU (at 4.1, the smoke run estimates 1,792 minutes for the full scope, so this job needs the GPU). A CUDA GPU in fp16 is expected to be much faster; at the
Apple rate the full scope would take about 3 hours, so the config stops at 110 minutes with `status:
stopped_time_limit` and a summary that records each stage's rate. If that happens, the fallback is a narrower rerun
(without or–en and `en-nemotroncc`), sized from that summary.

## CUDA

The command first prints the CUDA version and GPU name and stops at once if there is no CUDA GPU. `h3.py` then runs
every stage on the configured device (`"device": "cuda"`, no CPU fallback): the encoder is loaded on the GPU in fp16
(`precision: auto`) with TF32 matmuls, batches are moved from pinned memory without blocking, retrieval and sentence
alignment are matmuls on the device (`h3_align.py`), and each stage's result is copied to the host once.
`h3_summary.json` records the `device` and `precision` it used. Checked locally on CPU (the smoke run, 81 tests) and on
an Apple GPU (`device: mps`, `logs/2026-11-17-h3-local.txt`); the CUDA-only branches (pinned memory, fp16, TF32) can't
run on our machines, and the tests use a fake encoder on CPU.
