# RL sampler throughput and memory on Team 5's architecture (B5, 23 Nov 2026)

**Question.** How fast is our RL sampler on Team 5's dry run 2 model, how much memory does it use as rows in flight grow,
what share of an RL step is sampling, and does RL on the main model fit a 1-GPU job?
**Decides:** the sampler and rollout settings (prompts per step, k, max_new) and the time cap of the RL job request
(`jobs/2026-11-19-g10-rl-dryrun1.md`), and whether sampling or the update is the part to speed up first.

Every number below is printed from `b5_sampler_2026-11-23/raw/` by `python capstone/b5_tables.py results/b5_sampler_2026-11-23/raw`
(output kept in `b5_sampler_2026-11-23/tables.txt`). The question, explanations, endpoints and decision rule were written
before any timing (preregistration, 23 Nov 12:35 IST, kept with the job's working files).

## Setup
- **Model:** Team 5's `week2/model.py` at lab main `f360a794`, dry run 2 arm as resolved by `train_week2.py`
  (`b5_sampler_2026-11-23/spec_dryrun2_t2.json`): T2, 8 layers, width 512, 8 heads / 2 KV heads, 16 routed + 1 shared expert
  (hidden 768, top-2), vocab 131,072, context 2,048, **237,052,416 parameters**. T3 (`configs/arms/t3.yaml`: 10 layers, width
  640, expert hidden 896, **391,529,600 parameters**) is the largest Team 5 tier in the configs; both fit a 16 GB T4.
  Weights are Team 5's random init (seeded): **throughput and memory only, never a quality number**. Stop is disabled, so every
  row runs to max_new (the worst case for the time cap).
- **Sampler as the RL job runs it today:** `rl_job_v1.py` writes `sampler: policy`, `model.kv_cache: true`, k 8, 16 prompts
  per step, max_new 1,536, bf16 autocast over fp32 masters. `rollout.PolicySampler` calls `g5_decode.sample` **once per
  prompt**, so a step decodes 16 groups of 8 rows one after another.
- **Step 1 check:** `g5_decode.check_supported` passes on main's dry run 2 model (all 8 layers global, no sink, no gate, no
  MTP). Parity with Team 5's full forward on the CPU stand-in checkpoint: 2.1e-6 (`raw/step1_check.log`).
- **Hardware:** Colab Tesla T4 (15 GB, torch 2.14.1+cu130, python 3.13), and Apple M1 Pro CPU (torch 2.14.1). The T4 has no
  native bf16, so its main arms use fp16 autocast as the throughput stand-in; bf16 and fp32 arms are recorded too.
- **Timing:** one warm-up call per arm; the device is synchronised before every clock read; a decode timing covers the
  whole `sample` / `sample_many` call (prefill included); the step split times each module separately (median of 20 synced
  calls); the update times train.py's own `chunked_logprobs` and `backward_objective_v1` + clip + AdamW.

## What this PR changes (our files only)
1. `rl-loop/g5_decode.py`: `sample_many`, which decodes **every prompt of a step in one call**. Each prompt is prefilled once
   (left padding to the longest prompt) and its cache is copied to its k rows. RoPE positions count from each row's first
   real token, and our attention masks the padding. The cache is preallocated (no `torch.cat`). Grouped-head attention reads
   each K/V group directly, so there is no `repeat_interleave` copy of the cache per step, and no fp32 copy of it under
   autocast, where the matmul runs in the autocast dtype anyway. `parity_many` checks every row against Team 5's forward. The
   per-prompt `sample` and `_attend` are unchanged.
2. `rl-loop/rollout.py`: sampler kind `g5_batch` (chunks of at most `sampler_max_rows`, default 128). `team5_adapter.py`:
   `Bundle.sample_many` and `Team5Policy.sample_many` (same checks and autocast as `sample`). `train.py`: accepts `g5_batch`
   for the G5 model; the default stays `policy`, so no existing run changes.
3. `capstone/bench_rl_step_g5.py` (the bench), `capstone/b5_tables.py` (tables from raw), `rl-loop/test_g5_sample_many.py`
   (5 tests on the COMMON CPU stand-in).

## Results (T4 unless marked; T2 fp16; stop disabled)

**Planned RL step: 16 prompts x k 8 x 1,536 new tokens** (prompt lengths 195/300/414/512, the train pool's p10, a value
between, its p50 and the 512 cap that max_new 1,536 leaves):
| sampler | seconds | tok/s | r = s per prompt's 8 samples | peak GB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `policy` (today: one call per prompt) | 708.48 | 277.5 | 44.28 | 1.621 |
| `g5_batch` before the attention change (run 1 / repeat) | 145.04 / 153.94 | 1,355.6 / 1,277.2 | 9.06 / 9.62 | 3.383 |
| **`g5_batch` (this PR)** | **55.27** | **3,557.5** | **3.45** | **2.254** |

12.8x against today's sampler at the job's settings. The attention change alone is 2.79x on the same T4 and command (before
and after interleaved; the before arm repeated 6% slower than its first run, which is the drift on this T4).

**Rows in flight** (512 new, same prompts for `policy` and `g5_batch`):
| rows | `policy` tok/s | `g5_batch` before the attention change | `g5_batch` (this PR) | peak GB (this PR) | ms per decode step (this PR) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 276.0 | 276.4 | 271.1 | 1.173 | 29.5 |
| 64 | 274.5 | 1,510.3 | 1,976.3 | 1.635 | 32.4 |
| 128 | 275.3 | 1,828.7 | 3,738.1 | 2.136 | 34.2 |
| 256 | 274.9 (476.8 s call) | 2,016.5 | 5,620.1 | 3.137 | 45.6 |
| 512 | stopped (call over 240 s) | 2,127.7 | 6,617.3 | 5.140 | 77.4 |
One call of k = R rows of a single prompt (`sample`) ran out of memory at 512 rows on the T4 (15 GB). It prefills the same
prompt R times through Team 5's all-experts MoE; at 256 rows its peak was already 14.3 GB. `sample_many` prefills each
prompt once (5.1 GB at 512 rows).

**T3 (391.5M), 128 rows, 512 new:** `policy` 206.1 tok/s, `g5_batch` before the attention change 1,186.9 tok/s (5.76x). The
attention change was not re-run on T3.

**What limits a decode step** (preregistered explanations E1 to E5):
- **E1 (per-prompt loop): confirmed, first bottleneck.** `policy` tok/s is flat in rows (274 to 276 from 8 to 256 rows), so
  P prompts take P times as long.
- **E5 (fixed per-step overhead): confirmed at small rows.** A step costs 26 to 29 ms at 1 to 16 rows, flat in position
  (8 rows: first 10% median 26.3 ms, last 10% 26.4 ms). The module split at 1 row gives about 1.6 ms of attention and
  1.8 ms of MoE per layer. The MoE part is Team 5's `torch.stack` of all 16 experts' weights plus a dtype cast on every call,
  the same at 1 row as at 8.
- **E2 (cache `torch.cat`): small.** 1 to 7% of a step in fp16 (10% in fp32) at every size measured (128 rows, cache 1,952: cat 1.16 ms of 16.4 ms
  attention per layer). What grew with position before the change was the attention's own copies (`repeat_interleave` x4
  and an fp32 cast of the whole cache per layer per step): at 128 rows the step went from 55 ms (median of the first 10% of positions) to 147 ms
  at position 1,950, with attention 86% of the step at cache 1,952. This PR removes those copies (above).
- **E3 (the 131,072-row head): not the bottleneck.** 0.6 to 0.9 ms per step at 1 to 128 rows in fp16 (2 to 3%); 8.0 ms
  under bf16 on the T4 (no native bf16).
- **E4 (MoE dispatch): Team 5's MoE runs all 16 routed experts on every token** (dense einsum): 160M multiply-adds per token
  in T2 against 28M for top-2 routing (`raw/param_breakdown.txt`). In decode at these sizes it costs 1.8 to 3.2 ms per layer
  (weight copies dominate), 4 to 50% of a step depending on rows. In training forward and backward it multiplies the expert
  compute by 5.7. Team 5's module: not edited here.

**The trainer's update** (one group of 8 rows x 1,952 tokens, train.py's own functions, fp16 autocast, AdamW on everything
except the routers, 236,986,880 trainable): micro rows 8, 4 and 2 run out of memory on 15 GB. Micro rows 1: old
log-probs 1.143 s, forward + backward 2.915 s, clip + AdamW 0.085 s, peak 9.76 GB. The memory peak is the
`[rows, T, 131,072]` fp32 logits in `completion_logprobs`: 1.9 GiB per row at 1,952 tokens, so 15 GiB for the 8 rows of a
group when `micro_rows` is unset, as `rl_job_v1.py` leaves it. T3 at 928 tokens: micro 2 fits (12.0 GB), 4 does not.

| clip + AdamW, once | 0.1 s | 0.1 s | measured |
| scoring, 128 programs | 8 s | 8 s | **not measured here**: the job request's basis (0.5 s per prompt's 8 samples, E1 pool) |

So with today's sampler, sampling is the part to speed up, and this PR does it. After it, on this GPU the update (65 s) is
the larger part. The scoring basis was not measured on a GPU host, and on 15 Nov the verifier was 70% of a step until it was
pooled; it must be measured on the job's host.

## Extrapolation (estimates, not measurements)
**T2 on one H200 (dry run 2 size).** Model of a decode step: t(R) = F + c*R. From the T4 sweep, F is about 29 ms (fixed:
kernel launches and Team 5's per-call expert-weight copies) and c is about 0.11 ms per row above 128 rows. Assumptions: c
scales with memory bandwidth (4.8 vs 0.32 TB/s, so 15x smaller); F lies between unchanged (host-bound) and a third of its T4
value. The update is 5 to 15x faster than on the T4 (bf16 tensor cores, micro rows 8 fit in 141 GB). Scoring stays 8 s.
| | `policy` | `g5_batch` |
|---|---|---|
| r (s per prompt's 8 samples at 1,536) | 15 to 45 | 1.1 to 2.9 |
| minutes per RL step | 4.3 to 12.2 | 0.5 to 1.1 |
| steps per 8 h | 39 to 112 | 430 to 990 |
The job request's time table (`jobs/2026-11-19-g10-rl-dryrun1.md`) goes up to r = 9.4 s. With `policy`, r on a T4 is 44 s,
and on the H200 it is 15 to 45 s. At r above about 22 s the job's own `enough_time` rule (screen 64 + 3 updates + the last
evaluation after the 28-minute SFT rebuild) takes the screen-only branch, and 10 updates (16 prompts each) at r = 30 s need
about 84 minutes. With `g5_batch` the 10 updates need about 7 to 12 minutes (the table's own 16 s update and 0.5 s scoring
bases included). The job measures r with a per-prompt rate probe,
so switching the sampler also needs the rate probe to time the batched path. The patch is in
`b5_sampler_2026-11-23/rl_job_patch.diff` (against #139's head); the job request is ours to change once the lead agrees.

**30B to 120B main model on one H200 (141 GB).**
- **Full-parameter RL with this trainer does not fit.** fp32 master + fp32 gradient + AdamW is 16 bytes per trainable
  parameter: 480 GB at 30B. One H200 holds about 8.8B trainable parameters before activations. RL on the main model in a
  1-GPU job means a frozen base (LoRA, Deletion Tuning, head-only) or more GPUs.
- **Frozen base, bf16 weights about 60 GB** (Session 18 quotes about 62 GB for a 30B example), leaving about 70 GB for the
  KV cache, activations and logits.
- **KV cache per row** at 2,048 slots is 2 x layers x KV heads x head dim x 2 bytes x 2,048. With Session 18's generic 30B
  figure (384 KB per token), that is 768 MiB per row: 128 rows (k 8 x 16 prompts) need 96 GiB and do **not** fit; about 64
  rows do. With Team 5's 2 KV heads (as in T2 and T3) at, say, 48 layers and head dim 128, it is 96 MiB per row: 128 rows
  need 12 GiB and fit. Which one holds depends on the main model's shape, which Team 5 fixes after corpus v1's token count.
- **Update memory:** the `[rows, T, 131,072]` logits are 1.9 GiB per row whatever the model size, so `micro_rows` must be set
  (at most about 4 to 8 rows next to a 30B frozen base).
- **Time per RL step with `g5_batch`**, assuming Team 5's all-experts MoE stays as it is: about 170 GB of weight traffic per
  decode step (the expert stack copy plus the reads) is about 35 ms, plus 2 x 30B x 128 rows of compute (8 to 15 ms), so 45
  to 60 ms per step and 70 to 90 s of sampling. The update is about 6e16 FLOP (old log-probs plus forward and backward
  through the frozen base, 128 x 1,952 tokens), 85 to 150 s at 400 to 700 TFLOPS. Scoring 8 s. **Total 2.7 to 4.1 minutes
  per step, about 115 to 175 steps per 8 hours.** With `policy`, each of the 16 prompts pays the weight traffic per token:
  14 to 18 minutes of sampling, about 23 to 30 steps per 8 hours.
- At 30B with `g5_batch`, sampling is about 35 to 50% of a step. The update through the all-experts MoE becomes the main
  cost. A top-2 dispatch in Team 5's model would cut its expert compute by about 5.7x (their module; a note to Team 5, not
  an edit here).

## What does not transfer
T4 (Turing, no native bf16, 15 GB) vs H200 (bf16 tensor cores, 141 GB, 4.8 TB/s); fp16 on the T4 vs bf16 on the job's GPU;
random-init T2/T3 weights vs a trained checkpoint. Completion length is a property of the weights; here every row runs to
1,536, while real rows that stop early save `policy` more than `g5_batch`, because a batch runs until its longest row
stops. Other gaps: T2/T3 vs a 30B to 120B model; the dense head vs the coming KE2/KAS head; the Colab VM's CPU vs TSAI's
host (the fixed per-step overhead is host-bound). One run per arm, one seed; drift between repeats of the same arm was 6%.

## Not measured
Scoring time on a GPU host; any H200, H100 or A100 timing; bf16 on a GPU with native bf16; the attention change on T3; a
real RL step through `train.py` on the T4 (the step share above sums measured parts); trained weights; real completions that
stop early.
