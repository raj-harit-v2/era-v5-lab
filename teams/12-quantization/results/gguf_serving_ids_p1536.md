# GGUF round trip, serving with token ids: tokenizer p1536

Generated by `tokenizer/serving_ids_check.py` on 2026-11-18 (arm64 Darwin). Do not edit by hand; rerun the script.

**Question.** `gguf_roundtrip_p1536.md` fails because llama.cpp's own tokenizer differs from tokenizer.json: the GGUF cannot carry p1536's normalizer, and llama.cpp's split pattern tells capitals from lower case with ASCII ranges only. Does the round trip pass when the serving app tokenizes with tokenizer.json and sends llama.cpp token ids (llama-server `/completion` with `prompt` as a list of ids, `/detokenize` for the text)? vLLM reads tokenizer.json itself, so it is on this path already.

## Result

- **Ids in, text out: 0 of 20,400 test lines differ**; the model ran on the ids in every set: yes. Same 22 sets and lines as `gguf_roundtrip_p1536.md`.
- **Whole vocabulary: 0 of 131,072 ids** have a different text in llama.cpp.
- **llama-server** (same GGUF): `/detokenize` differs on 0 of 440 lines; `/completion` with a 13-id prompt evaluated 13 tokens: OK.

**Verdict: PASS.** A serving app that tokenizes with tokenizer.json and sends ids gets exactly the training ids on every test line, and llama.cpp gives back the same text for every id. The normalizer and the split pattern stay in tokenizer.json, where they are exact.

**What it means.** The normalizer cost is not spread evenly. Where a pack's text uses the characters the normalizer rewrites, most documents reach llama.cpp in a form the model never saw in training; elsewhere almost none do. The split-pattern cost is small in every pack and comes from Latin, Greek or Cyrillic words with a non-ASCII capital. A plain-text client (Ollama, or llama-server with a text prompt) therefore needs the app to normalize first, and still keeps the split-pattern difference; a client that sends tokenizer.json's ids has neither.
